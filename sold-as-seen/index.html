Sold As Seen helps you check a used car before you go and see it: share the advert to the app, and it keeps the car on a shortlist with its official MOT history, the things worth asking about, what it costs to run, how far away it is and a score against what matters to you. It is built so that your shortlist stays with you. This page says exactly what leaves your phone, why, and what does not.

The short version

  • Your shortlist — the cars, what their adverts said, photos, notes, your budget and your scoring preferences — is stored on your phone. It is not sent to us. We have no copy.
  • If you want the app to fetch a car's MOT history, you create an account with an email address and a password. That is the only personal information we hold about you. It is the same account as Garaged, our vehicle maintenance app: one account works in both, and deleting it in either deletes it for both.
  • When you save a car with its registration number, that number is sent to the DVSA MOT History service to fetch the car's details and MOT history, and typed into DVLA's vehicle enquiry page to read its tax status and CO2. We do not keep a record of either.
  • If you enter your home postcode, it and the advert's location are sent to two map services to work out the distance and the drive. They are not sent to us.
  • Adverts are read on your phone, in the app's own browser, when you ask. Photos and screenshots are read on your phone and never uploaded.
  • There is no advertising, no analytics and no crash reporting in the app. The app never reads your phone's location.

What Why Legal basis

Accounts are hosted by Supabase Inc. in the United Kingdom (London, eu-west-2). Supabase acts as our processor. Their authentication service keeps security logs, which can include the IP address and device details of sign-in attempts, for the period set out in their own policy.

Why an account at all, if your shortlist stays on the phone? Because the vehicle lookup below spends a request against a government API quota issued in our name, and the service has to be able to tell one user from another to limit how often it is called. Nothing else about the account is used for anything. The rest of the app works without one.

One account for two apps. Sold As Seen and Garaged share the same account system. An account made in either app signs in to both. Garaged's own privacy policy, at privacy.seaembee.co.uk, covers what Garaged does with it.

Account email

The confirmation link sent when you sign up, and the reset link sent if you forget your password, are delivered by Resend, an email-sending service, on our behalf. Resend acts as our processor for this purpose only: it sees the address the mail is sent to and holds a short delivery log (whether the message was accepted, delivered or bounced) for the period set out in their own policy. It does not see anything else.

Vehicle lookup

When you save a car with its registration number, the app sends that number to a small service we run, which forwards it to the DVSA MOT History API and returns the car's make, model, colour, fuel type, engine size, registration dates, MOT test results, recorded mileages and whether DVSA has a recall outstanding for it. That data is then stored on your phone. The lookup runs once, when you save the car, and again only if you ask for it.

  • Our service does not store the registration number or the result. It holds a short-lived count of lookups per account, in memory, to limit how often the service can be called.
  • The hosting provider's function logs may record that a request happened, and when, for a short period.
  • The DVSA receives the registration number in order to answer the lookup. The DVSA is a government body and is a separate data controller for what it does with requests it receives.
  • MOT data is public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0, and is reproduced in the app with that attribution.

You can save a car without a registration number, and nothing is sent.

DVLA's vehicle enquiry

For a car's tax status, tax due date, CO2 figure and the date its logbook was last issued, the app opens DVLA's public vehicle enquiry page (vehicleenquiry.service.gov.uk) in a browser view inside the app, on your phone, enters the registration number and confirms the make, and reads the answer. This happens once for a car, when you first open its report, and again only when you tap Check with DVLA.

The request goes from your phone to GOV.UK directly, exactly as if you had visited the page yourself: GOV.UK sees your phone's internet address and the registration number. The DVLA is a separate data controller for that page, and GOV.UK's own privacy notice applies to it. Nothing from this check is sent to us; the answer is stored on your phone.

Distance and the drive

If you enter your home postcode in your profile, the app sends it to postcodes.io, a free postcode lookup service, to turn it into map coordinates, and does the same with the location in each car's advert. It then sends the two coordinates to the OpenStreetMap routing service run by FOSSGIS e.V. to get the driving distance and time. The results are stored on your phone and each route is asked for once.

Both are third-party services, and both see what is sent to them: a postcode or town, and a pair of coordinates. Neither is sent to us. Leave the postcode out and nothing is sent; the report then has no distance.

The weekly road fuel prices the app uses to cost the drive are a public statistics file from GOV.UK; fetching it sends nothing about you.

Adverts, in the app's browser

The app reads an advert in two ways, both on your phone: you find the car in the app's own browser and tap +, or you share the advert's link to the app from another app and it opens in that browser. Either way the page is loaded by your phone from the seller's website, as any browser would load it, and that website sees what any website sees: your phone's internet address, and a browser identification that names this app. The app reads the price, mileage, registration, description and photos off the page once, when you tap, and stores what it read on your phone. Refresh reads a saved advert again, only when you tap it.

No advert, page or photo is sent to us. There is no database of adverts anywhere but your phone.

Screenshots and text recognition

Share a screenshot of an advert to the app and it reads the text off the image on your phone, using Google's ML Kit text-recognition component, which is built into the app. The image and the recognised text never leave your phone for this purpose. Google's component may report usage statistics about itself to Google, under Google's own terms; no image and no text is included in that.

Everything else stays on your device

The following are stored only on your phone, in the app's private storage:

  • Your shortlist: each car's registration, what its advert said, the official record fetched for it, the analysis and the score
  • Photos and screenshots you shared to the app, or that it read from an advert
  • Your notes on each car, and where each car got to (viewed, rejected, bought)
  • Your buying projects: budget, how far you will travel, must-haves and deal-breakers, your miles a year
  • Your profile: home postcode, the car you drive to viewings, and how much each part of the score matters to you

None of this is transmitted to us. The app has no server-side copy of your shortlist.

Sharing a report

You can share a car's report as a single file, through Android's share sheet, to a recipient you choose. It is produced on your phone. It carries the car's details, its MOT history, the analysis and the score, and says the score was made for you; it leaves out the distance from your home. We never receive a copy.

What we do not do

  • We do not use advertising or advertising identifiers.
  • We do not use analytics, tracking or crash-reporting services.
  • We do not read your phone's location. The app has no location permission.
  • We do not sell, rent or share your personal information with anyone, except as described above for the vehicle lookup, DVLA's page and the map services.
  • We do not send marketing email. The only emails you receive are the ones you trigger: account confirmation and password reset.

How long we keep things

  • Your account, and the email address attached to it, until you delete the account.
  • Lookup service logs: for the short retention period applied by the hosting provider.
  • Your shortlist on the phone: until you delete the cars in the app, or uninstall the app, or clear its data.

Your rights

Under the UK GDPR you can ask us for a copy of the personal data we hold about you, ask us to correct it, ask us to delete it, and object to or restrict our processing of it. Because the only personal data we hold is your account details, these requests are simple to meet.

To exercise any right, contact aoo.garaged@gmail.com. We will respond within one month.

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account from inside the app, on the Profile screen. Deleting it removes the account, the email address it is under, and everything held against it on our server. Because the account is shared with Garaged, deleting it in Sold As Seen deletes it for Garaged too, and the other way round. This cannot be undone.

You can also delete your account without the app: contact aoo.garaged@gmail.com from the email address on the account, and we will remove it.

Either way, deleting your account does not touch the shortlist on your phone. That is yours to keep or remove. The registration lookup stops working until you create a new account, and the rest of the app keeps working on the cars already on the phone.

Children

Sold As Seen is for people buying a car. It is not directed at children, and we do not knowingly hold an account for anyone under 13.

Changes

If the app starts sending any of your shortlist to a server — for example if a paid vehicle history check is added in future — that will be a change to this policy, published with the version of the app that introduces it, and described in the app before it is switched on for you.
